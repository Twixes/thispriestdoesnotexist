# Paired latent-to-priest adaptation

2026-09-21. **Recommend a bounded supervised generator-edit experiment from the exact FFHQ1024 source, using edited clothing targets and original faces as separate supervision.** This removes the small target discriminator from training and gives each target a known latent identity. It is a stronger preservation hypothesis than the contracting unpaired runs, not established priest quality or generalization. No models were downloaded and no training/compute experiment was run for this review.

## Relevant primary methods

| Method | Evidence relevant here | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [JoJoGAN, ECCV 2022](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136760124.pdf), [official implementation](https://github.com/mchong6/JoJoGAN) | Directly fine-tunes StyleGAN from latent/reference pairs; supports multiple references; uses frozen pretrained discriminator activations as a perceptual loss and optionally an identity loss. This D is a feature extractor, not a trained adversary. | Its style-mixed pseudo-pairs can deliberately erase differences; the paper warns excessive mixing can map every face to the reference. We already have exact pairs, so omit that augmentation initially. Cartoon stylization evidence does not prove photorealistic clothing transfer. |
| [PTI paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05744), [official reconstruction loop](https://github.com/danielroich/PTI/blob/main/training/coaches/base_coach.py), [locality regularizer](https://github.com/danielroich/PTI/blob/main/criteria/localitly_regulizer.py) | Tunes G at fixed latent pivots with reconstruction losses; regularizes sampled nearby latents against the frozen original generator. Exact source latents eliminate our need for its inversion step. | PTI aims for local reconstructions, not a new globally consistent clothing domain. Full-image source preservation would oppose learning priest shirts; ours must preserve face/hair/background while allowing clothing changes. |
| [Rewriting Geometric Rules of a GAN, SIGGRAPH 2022](https://peterwang512.github.io/GANWarping/), [official code](https://github.com/PeterWang512/GANWarping) | Learns shared model edits from a few edited generator outputs, including geometric and color edits. A low-rank update limits changed capacity. | Published augmentation reapplies a known warp to mixed samples. An imagegen clothing edit is not such a reusable warp; copying the old target onto new mixed identities would create false supervision. Low-rank adaptation is a possible second experiment, not necessary for the first. |

This proposed method combines these ideas; it is not a faithful reproduction of any one paper and is not CLIP-NADA. No diffusion model runs during training or serving: built-in imagegen supplies offline paired targets only.

## Minimal concrete experiment

1. Prepare **50 reviewed distinct adult-male pairs**, with varied identities, hair, beard, age, pose and visible neck/clothing. Reserve ten entire seed identities as edited validation; train on forty. Expand toward 100–200 only after an unseen-seed benefit. Keep a manually checked clothing mask per pair, with a narrow transition band; reject edits that move heads or alter facial anatomy. Preserve original color sources, even if targets are monochrome.
2. Initialize the student from the exact source serving G. Freeze mapping, W average, constant noise and synthesis blocks through 32px; initially tune synthesis blocks 64–1024. This is a proposed capacity constraint, not a proven semantic boundary. It may underfit a collar, but leaves coarse identity structure less freedom. Keep source G frozen/eval; use unmixed original W and constant noise. Apply a fixed differentiable grayscale conversion to outputs and sources during losses and serving so G need not relearn global monochrome appearance.
3. For a paired seed, supervise **clothing pixels against the edit** and **all protected pixels against grayscale source**. Normalize each masked L1 by its mask area so the small collar is not drowned by the face. Add a modest frozen-source-D feature loss on the clothing region: compare images that share the same protected source pixels and differ only inside the clothing mask. This uses compositing only as a loss input; the delivered image must be the student's complete generated output. Use feature blocks before minibatch statistics and document the chosen layers; do not train D, use adversarial loss, R1, or PL here. Pixel loss alone is a useful baseline but may blur detailed collars.
4. Add source preservation on fresh unpaired latents, in a **protected upper-face/hair region**, comparing student to frozen grayscale source. A conservative fixed region is dependency-free but misses lower-face drift and varies with framing; record that limitation and evaluate full faces. Better per-source face masks require an independently validated mask source. Never penalize the entire fresh image toward its original clothes. Sequential paired and preservation backward passes before one optimizer update bound memory. An initial low LR (e.g. Adam 1e-4) is an experiment choice, not a cited optimum.
5. First run only a ten-update batch-1/FP32 correctness and memory smoke with one real paired loss and one fresh preservation loss per update. Verify source/frozen blocks unchanged, finite gradients, exact checkpoint/RNG resume and initial source reconstruction. If it fits with training-job coordination, budget **200 updates**, inspect at 25/50/100/200, and stop if facial fidelity falls. Preserve raw and EMA previews; never hide drift with tighter truncation.

## What would demonstrate progress

Show matched source/student rows for all ten held-out edited identities and **64 previously unseen, preselected adult-male seeds**, plus an unfiltered fresh random grid. Judge at 1024: facial anatomy/detail versus its own source, distinct identities, correct collar geometry, hats, preserved pose/background and nearest-training-neighbor resemblance. Record region-wise face drift and clothing improvement separately; global reconstruction loss can fall while the collar remains absent. A second untouched seed set is needed after selecting a checkpoint.

Fifty to two hundred pairs cover only a tiny latent region. A shared clothing rule may generalize, but memorized collars, face averaging, mask seams, and shirt failures on extreme poses remain plausible. Training-seed quality is not evidence of new identities. Source preservation also retains **women, children, hats, unattractive-to-the-owner faces and tight crops** in FFHQ. Adult/hot-male pair selection alone does not make an unconditional sampler adult/hot-male-only. An independently evaluated attribute-conditioned or rejection sampler would remain separate work, with rejection affecting serving cost; a finite approved-seed catalog would not meet the generative objective.

## Local cost, dependencies and licenses

The proposal reuses installed PyTorch/NumPy/Pillow and existing NVIDIA G/D weights. No inverter, ArcFace, diffusion checkpoint, or CLIP is required for the minimal version. Optional LPIPS/identity encoders introduce separate pretrained-weight provenance and memory. A frozen D still needs input-gradient computation, so it is not free.

On a 48 GB M5 Pro, batch-1 first-order supervised adaptation is **plausible**: it removes trainable D/its optimizer and all second-order regularization, and a frozen low-resolution prefix can avoid retaining those activations. But 1024 activations dominate; no measured MPS memory or step time exists for this recipe. The unrelated microbatch-8 256 Gmain probe exceeded a 4 GiB allocator cap, so do not infer fit from parameter sizes. Run the smoke without a third competing long job. If measured update time is t seconds, the initial 200-update budget is 200t plus snapshots; published CUDA timings are not local estimates.

Inference remains one adapted G plus grayscale conversion, keeping the tested CPU architecture. Existing FFHQ1024 Linux-emulated evidence is 4.48 s median warm generation and 1.50 GiB cgroup peak (`research/runs/inference-cpu/ffhq1024/docker-4g/README.md`); this is not native VPS latency or an adapted-model benchmark.
