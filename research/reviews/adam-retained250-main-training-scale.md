# Main-adaptation training scale: interpreting the first100 steps

2026-09-22. Read-only analysis of archived executable AdAM source and our native implementation; no model was loaded or training changed. The counts below come from the pinned source recipe, not a claim of paper-optimal priest hyperparameters. At this review the native10 run had completed and its continuation toward100 was active under root supervision.

**100 batch1 main-adaptation steps are an early stability/direction check, not a fair deadline for priest conversion.** Upstream requests1500 iterations at batch4 for FFHQ→Babies and2500 at batch4 for FFHQ→AFHQ-Cat: approximately6000/10,000 real-image presentations. Our100 at batch1 provides100, only1/60 of the smaller recipe's sample budget. The source Python loop actually adds10 iterations beyond its requested horizon. Matching6000 presentations at batch1 would require6000 iterations, but would not reproduce batch4 optimization or justify blindly running that long. Sources: [published recipes](../experiments/adam_native/upstream/source/_bash_main_adaptation.sh), lines10–24; [source loop](../experiments/adam_native/upstream/source/AdAM_main_adaptation.py), lines150–202; [native sampling](../experiments/adam_native/main_adaptation_fixed_offsets250_10.py), lines242–258.

Both implementations update G EMA once per iteration with `beta = 0.5**(32/10000)`, giving a312.5-iteration half-life. The original-parameter contribution is `beta**N`:

| Main iterations | Initial-parameter EMA coefficient |
|---:|---:|
|10|97.81%|
|50|89.50%|
|100|80.11%|
|250|57.43%|
|500|32.99%|
|1500|3.59%|

These are parameter coefficients, **not fractions of visible source imagery**. Modulation is bilinear, so EMA factors are not an image mixture or even an EMA of folded weights. There is no accidental longer EMA horizon in our implementation: batch1 makes the same312.5 updates correspond to312.5 real images, versus1250 at upstream batch4. Shortening EMA is therefore not an obvious correction. Compare raw and EMA separately before interpreting weak early EMA changes. Sources: [upstream EMA](../experiments/adam_native/upstream/source/AdAM_main_adaptation.py), lines170 and560–561; [native EMA](../experiments/adam_native/main_adaptation_fixed_offsets250_10.py), lines282–284; [modulation](../experiments/adam_native/modulation.py), lines44–49.

The strongest concrete batch1 limitation is D's minibatch-standard-deviation feature: at one image, subtracting the group mean yields zero, so the appended feature is always `sqrt(1e-8)=1e-4`. Its batch-diversity signal is absent. Higher gradient variance than batch4 is also expected, but its contribution to our observed learning speed has not been measured. Four single-image gradient-accumulation passes could improve gradient averaging **without restoring the minibatch feature**. A genuine batch2/4 comparison would need separate memory feasibility work. Sources: [native minibatch standard deviation](../vendor/stylegan2-ada-pytorch/training/networks.py), lines595–609; [upstream equivalent](../experiments/adam_native/upstream/source/gan_training/models/model_adam.py), lines1138–1145; [native D metadata](../models/ffhq1024-discriminator/metadata.json), lines36–37.

Capacity is uneven by design. Current q50 selection protects all original convolution rows at512 and1024, leaving those layers modulation-only; coarser layers retain many directly trainable rows. This follows a pooled percentile, not a50% allocation within each layer. Fixing34 synthesis noise/activation-bias offsets further limits adaptation freedom. Either may affect learning speed, but neither is demonstrated to prevent clerical clothing or domain conversion. The `1e-7` factor initialization is inherited from upstream, not a newly introduced mismatch. Sources: [actual G selection](../runs/adam-native1024-retained250-fixed-offsets-main-adaptation10-q50-v1/selection-G.json), masks beginning at line12127,1024conv0 at17696 and512conv0 at20596; [pooling](../experiments/adam_native/selection.py), lines219–230; [fixed-offset/mask policy](../experiments/adam_native/adaptation_masks_fixed_offsets.py), lines126–148; [native initialization](../experiments/adam_native/modulation.py), lines26–42; [upstream initialization](../experiments/adam_native/upstream/source/gan_training/models/model_adam.py), lines199–200 and432–433.

Native1024 versus source256 also increases image pixels16-fold and changes the architecture/work per iteration, but that does not imply a16-fold compute ratio or prescribe a learning-rate adjustment. The optimizer rates and lazy regularizer schedule themselves match the executable source. **Conditional next step:** if100 remains photographic but shows little priest conversion, continue the same trajectory through bounded250/500 visual reviews toward a1500-step development reference. Absence of conversion at100 alone should not trigger another reset. Photographic regression or collapsing diversity should prompt reassessment. The source-like EMA at100 is particularly weak evidence against eventual learning. Conversely, this scale analysis does not predict success or excuse repeated quality deterioration. Earlier probing updates do not count toward the main-adaptation horizon: main training deliberately reloaded original G/D, and probing supplied masks only. Any longer run remains subject to root's actual image review, memory/time guards and scheduling decision; nothing launches automatically.
