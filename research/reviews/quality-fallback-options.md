# Quality fallbacks after the failed 50-image pilot

Research date: 2026-09-21. **Keep the current aligned-110 FreezeD experiment as a bounded GAN attempt, then prefer a source-preserving GAN experiment before changing architecture.** If it still produces distorted or repeated faces, a trained SD1.5 domain LoRA combined with an LCM acceleration LoRA is the most plausible diffusion alternative for inexpensive CPU serving. It is not StyleGAN-like and must be presented as an explicit architecture change, not silently counted as fulfilling that requirement.

The user is right that the pilot images are unacceptable. More training steps, favorable losses, unique image hashes, or merely recognizable collars do not establish convincing portraits. The target is photorealistic, attractive adult male identities with intact facial anatomy, visible clerical collars, and no hats, sampled afresh per request.

## Evidence and local constraints

Read-only hardware inspection confirmed **Apple M5 Pro, 48 GB unified memory** (`hw.memsize=51539607552`). Existing local training uses its Apple GPU through MPS; that is local GPU compute, not CPU-only training or rented GPU capacity. No NADA or diffusion training benchmark was run for this review.

The project's real 256px GAN serving benchmarks are encouraging for deployment cost: local two-thread CPU warm median 0.108 s for unadapted FFHQ and 0.142 s for the rejected pilot, at roughly 751–769 MB peak RSS. A local one-CPU Linux x86-64 container under Apple emulation measured a 0.714 s warm median, with approximately 934 MB cgroup peak. These prove an affordable **runtime shape**, not acceptable pictures or native VPS latency. See `research/runs/inference-cpu/README.md` and the per-run benchmark JSONs.

Current first-party Hetzner pricing lists CX33 at **$9.99/month or €8.49/month before tax and IPv4**. The prepared hosting plan specifies four shared vCPUs and 8 GB RAM. That is plausibly below the requested $15 final monthly budget, subject to checkout tax/currency/IP total and actual stock. Larger-memory plans or metered GPU serving are not justified within this budget. The origin has not yet been provisioned. [Current price notice](https://docs.hetzner.com/general/infrastructure-and-availability/price-adjustment/), local `infra/README.md`.

## Source-preserving GAN route

StyleGAN-NADA pairs a frozen generator and adapted generator, and trains the image-space CLIP change toward a text-defined semantic direction. The official code also accepts a small target-image set. It preserves a GAN at inference; CLIP and the frozen source are training machinery. Its published example uses 301 iterations and batch 2, but its GPU results are not an M5 time estimate. The original repository uses CUDA and a different StyleGAN implementation, so importing it untouched is not the next action. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00946), [official repository and example](https://github.com/rinongal/StyleGAN-nada).

Our inference: this is a better quality-preservation hypothesis than allowing every face feature to drift freely under a tiny discriminator. It is nevertheless a poor reason to promise success. FFHQ contains other ages/genders and mostly tight face crops. A generic “priest” text direction can introduce stereotyped headwear, alter the face, or fail to show the collar; identity preservation can also preserve unwanted source attributes. CLIP loss must never substitute for viewing the samples.

The existing CDC-only helper already gives a smaller next experiment than a full NADA port. Its actual batch-4 256px CPU diagnostic yielded zero loss for identical models, positive loss and finite gradients after target perturbation, and unchanged frozen-source state. It took 1.804 s for the extra forward/backward and reached 3.333 GiB cumulative process RSS. This is correctness/performance evidence only. See `research/experiments/cdc/README.md`. Strong correspondence might impede necessary framing changes, so it needs a matched visual ablation.

**Bounded next GAN experiment:** use a fresh FFHQ initialization and the same fixed 64 latent seeds; compare the aligned FreezeD baseline with CDC on otherwise identical settings at 250 and 500 updates. Inspect raw G and EMA at psi 1.0. If the face prior still collapses, prepare a 10-update NADA-style portable backward smoke, then at most 100–300 updates with a frozen source and one CLIP encoder, with samples at 25/50/100. Use paired source/target rows to expose exactly which identities or facial details deteriorated. Retain the same seeds and a fresh-seed grid. These are proposed experiments, not launched jobs.

## Trained fast diffusion alternative

A domain/style LoRA should represent the whole priest-photo distribution, not a single identity. Use the 110 fictional portraits with captions distinguishing age, hair, beard, pose and background; avoid assigning every example one person-like identity token. Reserve a fixed held-out set and compare nearest training neighbors. Full-model fine-tuning on so few faces is not the first choice.

The official Diffusers text-to-image LoRA example includes MPS handling (disabling native AMP), gradient checkpointing, and float32 trainable parameters. Start with batch 1, ordinary AdamW, frozen text encoder/VAE/base U-Net, and no CUDA-specific xFormers/bitsandbytes assumptions. A 10–20-step actual backward run is the feasibility gate; only its measured time and peak memory should set a 300–600-step pilot budget. Attention caching or precomputed latents can be later optimizations. [Official LoRA guide](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/main/en/training/lora), [official training implementation](https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/main/examples/text_to_image/train_text_to_image_lora.py), [MPS guidance](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/main/en/optimization/mps).

Train the domain LoRA against a normal compatible SD1.5 model first, evaluate normal sampling, then combine it with the existing LCM-LoRA for 4- and 8-step evaluation. This distinguishes poor domain learning from quality lost to acceleration. The LCM authors publish a 67.5M-parameter SD1.5 acceleration adapter for 2–8 steps; Diffusers documents combining acceleration and style LoRAs. Do not train an LCM distillation teacher from scratch: that is a much larger task and is unnecessary for this first comparison. [Author model card](https://huggingface.co/latent-consistency/lcm-lora-sdv1-5), [official combination guide](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/main/en/using-diffusers/inference_with_lcm_lora).

For CPU serving, cache text embeddings for a small prompt vocabulary (not completed images), fuse adapters where supported, and still draw new latent noise for every request. Batch 1 is the relevant benchmark. Run the actual final 512px pipeline in a four-CPU/8 GB container with 16 warm requests and four two-request bursts; record p50/p95, startup, RSS/cgroup peak, image defects and queue delay. Proposed practical acceptance targets are p95 at most 8 s and peak memory below 6.5 GiB, leaving OS/runtime headroom. Those targets are product decisions, not measured performance or original user requirements. If it cannot meet them, longer queues or stronger hardware would be an explicit tradeoff, not an invisible implementation detail.

There is no defensible primary-source latency number for this exact pipeline on the proposed shared VPS. Advertised GPU or modern desktop-CPU results cannot establish that it will feel like thispersondoesnotexist.com. SD1.5 FP32 weights are already several GB, unlike the roughly 100 MB GAN generator; even if inference fits, CPU arithmetic can dominate. SDXL is a fallback quality comparison, not a default sub-$15 deployment plan.

SD-Turbo is a distilled SD2.1 model with 512px one-step inference. Its model card explicitly states that faces/people can be generated incorrectly and recommends the larger SDXL-Turbo for quality. That makes it unsuitable as a confident answer to a complaint about malformed faces. A base-model 32-seed visual benchmark would precede any training investment, and normal denoising LoRA training must not be assumed to preserve its one-step behavior. [Primary model card](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/sd-turbo).

## Judge portraits, not just losses

For every candidate, show a complete predetermined 64-seed grid, plus larger individual crops and nearest-neighbor comparisons, with no cherry-picking. Record facial anatomy defects, visible-collar failures, hats, non-adult/non-male appearance, repeated identity clusters, and photographic credibility. Compare variation in face shape, hair, age and expression separately from changing backgrounds. An automated face detector or unique byte hashes cannot prove identity diversity. A frozen perceptual/identity metric can help locate near-duplicates, but a human-readable grid remains necessary.

As a proposed pilot gate, require at least 60/64 convincing intact faces and collars, no hats or apparent children, and no obvious one-face cluster before investing in a longer run. A deployment decision needs a larger fresh-seed sample and per-request failure handling; this pilot threshold is not a release guarantee. Source-preserving methods must be judged on whether the retained identities are still appropriate adults, and diffusion must be judged on whether low-step speed destroys the very face quality sought.
