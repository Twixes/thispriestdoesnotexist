# Restoration plus rejection: a plausible pipeline, not a quality result

2026-09-21. **Recommend one bounded GFPGAN v1.4 experiment on the complete saved raw step-625 32-image set after the current CPU training finishes.** It can establish whether the intact, painted-looking faces become convincing at the intended display size before further GAN training or a diffusion architecture change. There is no need to require every raw GAN sample to pass first: a validated combination of fresh GAN inference, rejection and restoration can satisfy the product. No restoration code or weights were installed or loaded for this note; only local text/filesystem metadata and primary documentation were read.

This updates `face-restoration-options.md`, `quality-fallback-options.md`, `post-factorial-fallback-feasibility.md` and `few-shot-options.md`. Earlier raw-output/no-compositing restrictions protected particular experiments; they are not user requirements. Preserve the raw evidence, but evaluate the final pipeline on its own merits. The final picture would come from our trained StyleGAN and a separately pretrained face prior, not a fixed portrait catalog and not a natively trained 1024 priest generator.

## What is plausible

The matched step-625 review found six clear severe head/face failures, one borderline architectural cheek merger, three headwear-like appearances, one definite extra partial head and one uncertain silhouette. Thirty images have legible clerical tabs; many intact faces have glassy eyes, weak lips and painted skin. The latter are plausible restoration targets. A missing scalp or columns replacing a face is a different structural failure. Restoration may hallucinate a plausible central face while leaving a malformed crown, extra person or broken collar outside its crop. These must still be rejected. This is an inference from our PNGs and model designs, not a tested repair rate. See `reference625-matched32-visual-review.json` (SHA256 `3423771062c2b7a8155d21530993cbe8ec066fe5d439a88569d5c3d493477bdb`).

Review the full image at 1024, including neck seams and texture consistency, rather than judging an attractive 512 face crop alone. Exact source identity is not required by the user, but plausible distinct faces remain required.

## Rejection is an engineering task, not a detector-confidence switch

Fresh independent latent draws followed by rejection still generate a new image for each successful request. A stored accepted-seed list or cached finished-image bank would not. Gate the **whole raw portrait before restoration**, then check the final portrait too. Face detection/landmark success alone does not test hats, scalp integrity, extra people, priest collars, adulthood or the subjective calendar aesthetic. Our existing YuNet is useful for alignment, not evidence of that eligibility. Restorer confidence is not an eligibility score either.

The current 32 review is suitable diagnostic ground truth, but too small and already inspected to validate a tuned rejector. An eventual fixed rejector needs independent human-reviewed random samples, separate error counts for false acceptance and false rejection, retained rejected outputs, and end-to-end acceptance/latency measurements. Do not silently accept raw failures because restoration makes the eyes sharper. Conversely, rejectors need not require preserving a particular FFHQ identity. No existing local restoration or rejection result establishes this gate yet.

For independent attempts with acceptance probability q, mean attempts are 1/q and a maximum K attempts has failure probability (1−q)^K. These are planning equations, not measured q; “26/32 not clearly severe” is **not** a quality acceptance estimate. A finite retry budget must fail without serving a known failed image. Include retry and queue costs in latency, and avoid a filter that simply narrows every request to one face family.

That cannot predict combined generator+detector+parser+restorer memory or native VPS latency. An 8 GB CPU origin is a plausible benchmark envelope, not a fit guarantee; a 3 GB origin is especially uncertain. Use one resident worker and serialize requests until concurrent peaks are measured.

Record per-stage cold initialization and every warm latency, peak process memory, alignment/restoration errors and RNG settings. Human-review all 32 native final pictures for face/head/ear/teeth integrity, collar, hats, extra people, blending seams, repeated face families and actual photographic credibility. The known raw catastrophic cases remain rejected controls, even if central-face restoration looks attractive. Do not tune on a few successful examples or promote this rejected checkpoint from sharpness alone.

**Only a convincing improvement justifies an end-to-end serving benchmark:** freeze restoration and rejection settings, generate a new predetermined 64-latent stream through actual G→raw gate→restorer→final gate→WebP, retaining every attempt and decision. Measure a one-worker Linux 8 GB pipeline including retries, cold start and a small two-request burst; human-audit all accepted and rejected images to expose the gate's errors. If the gate is still manual, label acceptance as an oracle upper bound, not automated service readiness. A 64-sample pilot cannot guarantee failure-free production, and byte-distinct outputs cannot establish novel identities. The immediate 32-image experiment answers whether restoration is useful enough to warrant that work; no installation, inference or serving change was performed in this review.
