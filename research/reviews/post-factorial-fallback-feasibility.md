# Conditional fallback after a four-arm supervised-fit failure

2026-09-21. Planning only; no model downloads/loads, compute jobs, purchases, credentials, or active-code changes. **If all four arms fail native supervised collar structure, the most useful alternative to investigate is a small diffusion prior plus a domain LoRA, clearly described as a different architecture with the same fresh-generation function.** First test its quality/CPU-serving feasibility; do not commit to LoRA training or promise the hosting budget from parameter counts.

**The user requires convincing new adult male priest portraits, not preservation of a particular FFHQ identity.** Exact source faces and frozen coarse blocks were our experimental safeguards, not user requirements. A fallback may change identity, pose and framing if it produces coherent photorealistic faces, visible collars, diversity and no hats.

This updates `quality-fallback-options.md` and `paired-edit-adaptation.md`, rather than repeating their earlier CDC/paired proposals. We now have exact paired 1024 training and frozen-D backward evidence: the 0.05 feature smoke completed at 8.28 GiB peak and 5.94 s per ordinary CPU update, while the fixed 1.0 factorial is underway. Those measurements do not predict whole-generator, CLIP or diffusion costs. A four-arm failure would rule out this particular six-pair/300-update/block-range/objective attempt, not every GAN method.

## What changes, and what still satisfies the request

| Alternative | Concrete distinction and main risk | Trained StyleGAN-like model? | Serving consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole-synthesis paired adaptation | Open remaining low-resolution synthesis blocks and reconsider source-preservation losses. This removes our chosen capacity constraint; identity changes are acceptable, but distorted faces and loss of diversity are not. More free parameters alone are not evidence of a collar fix. | Yes: updated StyleGAN weights, fresh latent per request. | Same single-G architecture and existing CPU route. |
| StyleGAN-NADA | Directional CLIP adaptation of frozen/source and trainable G; automatic layer selection is available. The official implementation is **not simply “train every weight”**. Text semantics might supply a priest-clothing prior, but “priest” can change faces/headwear or miss a tiny Roman tab. No cited result demonstrates our photoreal target. | Yes. CLIP is training machinery, not a serving dependency. | Single adapted G after training. |
| Model rewriting | Change selected layer associations/rules, potentially generalizing a local edit. It needs a useful internal correspondence and edit specification; our edited pixels do not automatically identify a collar-generating rule. It is a distinct implementation/research project, not an existing helper switch. | Yes, provided changed network weights produce the full new images. | Usually the same G footprint after the edit. |
| SD1.5 domain LoRA, optionally fused with LCM-LoRA | Broad text/image clothing prior, learned small adapter and fresh noise. This avoids forcing FFHQ's tight face generator to discover shirts from six pairs. Small-data memorization, malformed faces and low-step detail loss remain plausible. | **No** if StyleGAN/GAN architecture is required. **Yes only to the broader functional requirement** of locally trained weights and new server-generated images. The architecture change must be stated plainly, without redefining literal StyleGAN. | U-Net plus VAE, repeated denoising, substantially larger CPU memory/compute. |

The first three retain source-distribution issues: FFHQ includes children, women, hats and cramped necks. Diffusion prompts also do not guarantee adult male, bareheaded or owner-perceived attractiveness. Any validated rejection sampler adds cost: with acceptance fraction q, mean attempts are 1/q. Neither a finite accepted-seed bank nor generated-image cache fulfills genuinely new generation on each request.

Primary grounding: [NADA official code](https://github.com/rinongal/StyleGAN-nada) and [layer-selection implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rinongal/StyleGAN-nada/main/ZSSGAN/model/ZSSGAN.py); [rewriting paper/project](https://rewriting.csail.mit.edu/) and [official implementation](https://github.com/davidbau/rewriting). Rewriting's demonstrated network-level edits support feasibility in principle, not priest-quality guarantees. [GANWarping](https://github.com/PeterWang512/GANWarping) uses reusable geometric edits; imagegen clothing changes are not known warps, so its augmentation cannot be copied with false correspondences.

## Local M5 Pro 48 GB feasibility

**GAN variants:** the existing source generator is a 127 MiB safetensors file. Even roughly five copies' worth for trainable FP32 weights, gradients, Adam moments and frozen source is under 1 GiB; this is only a weight-state estimate. Native1024 activations and input-gradient feature encoders dominate: our restricted-synthesis feature smoke already used 8.28 GiB. Opening early blocks retains additional graphs; adding CLIP changes them again. Batch1 first-order training on 48 GB is plausible, but no measured memory or M5 time exists for these variants. NADA's repository reports a CUDA-oriented setup tested on a 16 GB RTX3080 and a short example run, not an Apple benchmark. Portability and finite backward still need testing; CUDA timings cannot justify an M5 deadline.

**Diffusion:** current Diffusers' [LoRA guide](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/main/en/training/lora) trains only added U-Net attention adapters. Its [actual training script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/diffusers/main/examples/text_to_image/train_text_to_image_lora.py) has MPS handling and disables native AMP there. A conservative local configuration is 512px, batch 1, frozen VAE/text encoder/base U-Net, rank 4 adapters, ordinary AdamW, gradient checkpointing and no assumed CUDA xFormers/bitsandbytes support. This is a proposed configuration, not a completed compatibility test. The public main script currently requires a development Diffusers version; pin a coherent release/script pair instead of mixing latest snippets with installed packages.

Published component files total approximately **4.27 GB decimal FP32 weights**: [U-Net 3.44 GB](https://huggingface.co/stable-diffusion-v1-5/stable-diffusion-v1-5/tree/main/unet), [VAE 335 MB](https://huggingface.co/stable-diffusion-v1-5/stable-diffusion-v1-5/tree/main/vae), [text encoder 492 MB](https://huggingface.co/stable-diffusion-v1-5/stable-diffusion-v1-5/tree/main/text_encoder). FP16 files roughly halve that; file size is not peak working memory. LoRA avoids full-base Adam state, making a 48 GB batch 1 trial credible, but retained activations, dtype/kernel support and unified-memory pressure remain unmeasured. Use an explicit prospective 16 GiB process/driver-memory stop criterion and no competing training job; this limit is an experiment guard, not an expected usage claim. MPS documentation warns about memory pressure and discusses attention slicing for sub-64-GB systems; benchmark compatible attention implementations rather than assuming slicing improves every modern pipeline. [Official MPS guide](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/main/en/optimization/mps).

The [LCM authors' SD1.5 adapter](https://huggingface.co/latent-consistency/lcm-lora-sdv1-5) has 67.5M parameters and supports 2–8 denoising steps. [Diffusers documents composition with a style LoRA](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/main/en/using-diffusers/inference_with_lcm_lora). Train the domain adapter on ordinary diffusion supervision; evaluate normal sampling separately from accelerated inference. This does not require training a distillation teacher. The SD1.5 [model card](https://huggingface.co/stable-diffusion-v1-5/stable-diffusion-v1-5) explicitly reports imperfect photorealism and face-generation limitations, so it is an affordable feasibility candidate, not the presumed visual winner. Native 512 quality also must be judged at the site's actual displayed size; enlargement does not establish 1024 detail.

## CPU hosting and licenses

The existing exact FFHQ1024 container measured **4.48 s warm median and 1.503 GiB cgroup peak**, locally under amd64 emulation with one CPU. This supports a small CPU runtime shape but is neither native-VPS latency nor approved priest quality (`research/runs/inference-cpu/ffhq1024/docker-4g/README.md`). GAN adaptation or rewriting keeps this architecture.

Diffusion's roughly 4 GiB FP32 base weights alone leave little activation/runtime/concurrency headroom on an 8 GB origin. Adapter fusion and cached prompt embeddings can reduce overhead; neither is a cache of generated images. Four denoising steps still require four U-Net passes plus VAE decode. No verified primary measurement establishes this exact pipeline's latency or memory on our proposed shared CPU host, and no numerical speed estimate is warranted. A good Mac result does not establish x86 shared-VPS performance.

The current [Hetzner notice](https://docs.hetzner.com/general/infrastructure-and-availability/price-adjustment/) lists CX33 at $9.99/month excluding IPv4 and tax; the [product page](https://www.hetzner.com/cloud/cost-optimized/) supplies configuration/availability. It is a plausible sub-$15 **checkout candidate**, not a guaranteed all-in price or available machine. Shared CPU, queues and rejection attempts matter. An always-on rented GPU is not supported by this budget evidence. Existing Cloudflare frontend/CI can remain, with generation on a separately budgeted personal CPU origin.

## One conditional next action

**If all four arms fail supervised structure, run one bounded SD1.5 feasibility gate before investing in a domain LoRA run, explicitly naming the architecture change.** This recommendation treats “StyleGAN-like” as trained fresh portrait generation; it does not claim diffusion is a GAN. Source-face identity matching is not part of its acceptance criteria. Fix a prompt and 32 unseen seeds in advance: realistic monochrome head-and-shoulders clearly adult male portraits, black clerical shirt/white Roman tab, bare head; retain every output. Compare normal sampling with one fixed four-step LCM setting, with no face restoration or output compositing. Record facial/neck artifacts, collar failures, hats and diversity at display size; a prompt-only success is not completion of local training.

Within that same gate, permit only 20 batch 1 LoRA updates on captioned training portraits to measure actual local backward time/memory/checkpoint reload, not claim learned quality. Benchmark the accelerated pipeline with one request at a time under a four-CPU/8-GiB Linux cap. Stop on resource failure or unacceptable faces. Proposed serving acceptance remains the earlier engineering target of p95≤8s and cgroup peak≤6.5GiB, with a separately reported two-request queue; these are not measured guarantees or user-specified latency requirements. Keep a 45-minute total budget and report incomplete measurements rather than extending it. No larger LoRA run or new hosting follows automatically.

If the user requires literal StyleGAN architecture rather than the stated fresh-generation behavior, this diffusion gate is out of scope; do not silently count it as fulfilling the goal. This memo recommends no additional automatic whole-G/NADA/rewriting experiment: those remain hypotheses with good serving economics but insufficient evidence of a better supervised collar solution. All sources were checked on 2026-09-21; no factorial outcome is assumed.
